The Most Rev. Michael Bertiaux (Tau Ogdoade-Orfeo IV) (born January 18, 1935) is an iconic American occult philosopher, theurgist, artist, and independent Gnostic bishop whose visionary corpus transformed modern esoteric cosmology[cite: 2]. Based for decades in Chicago, Illinois, Bertiaux is internationally celebrated as the architect of the Voudon-Gnostic current and author of the legendary monumental compendium The Voudon Gnostic Workbook (1988)[cite: 2].

Philosophical Formation & The Haitian Initiation

Unlike occultists who rely upon sensationalism, Bertiaux's foundation was deeply academic and theologically rigorous[cite: 2]. Educated under Jesuit fathers at Seattle University, with advanced graduate work in philosophy and theology at Tulane and the University of British Columbia, his work was heavily informed by the phenomenology of Edmund Husserl, Ernst Cassirer, Ludwig Wittgenstein, and G.W.F. Hegel, alongside Carl Jung[cite: 2].

  • Anglican Ministry & Culture Transition (Early 1960s): Graduating with honors from an Anglican seminary, Bertiaux served as curate of an Episcopal parish in West Seattle before receiving a commission to teach philosophy at the Anglican Church College in Port-au-Prince, Haiti, preparing for the cultural transition under the renowned anthropologist Margaret Mead[cite: 2].
  • The Haitian Consecration (August 15, 1963): While in Port-au-Prince, Bertiaux was introduced into the esoteric circles surrounding Hector-François Jean-Maine (Tau Ogdoade-Orfeo II)[cite: 2]. Initiated into the internal theurgical rites of La Couleuvre Noire (The Black Snake), Bertiaux was consecrated to the episcopate within the Neo-Pythagorean Gnostic Church, succeeding as Tau Ogdoade-Orfeo IV to serve as representative for Caucasian-American members[cite: 2].
  • The Theosophical & Chicago Milieu (1964–1970s): Relocating to Illinois, Bertiaux worked for the Theosophical Society in America at Wheaton, collaborating closely with Liberal Catholic and independent bishops including Dr. Stephan A. Hoeller and Bishop Gregory of the Russian Orthodox tradition[cite: 2]. In Chicago, while serving professionally as a state social work supervisor in Woodlawn, he developed the Monastery of the Seven Rays as an operative theurgical curriculum[cite: 2].
  • Psionic Machines & Pataphysical Art: Bertiaux integrated esoteric philosophy with visual art, radionics, and what he termed "trans-yuggothian transmission stations", asserting that all art forms function as operative psychotronic machines and doorways into the unconscious[cite: 2].

Luciferianism, the Neo-Luciferian Church, & The Ben Kadosh Question

Throughout his writings and interviews, Michael Bertiaux distinguished between crude popular misconceptions of devil-worship and the authentic Luciferian impulse[cite: 2]. He consistently framed Luciferianism as an essential, therapeutic catalyst of creative liberation: an artistic and psychological rebellion against the repressive guilt, fear, and dogmatic straightjackets of fundamentalist religious conditioning[cite: 2].

This focus made Bertiaux a foundational figure of inspiration for European esoteric circles, most notably the Neo-Luciferian Church (NLC) established in Scandinavia by Bjarne Salling Pedersen and his collaborators[cite: 2]. The NLC conducted in-depth interviews with Bertiaux, published his treatises, and integrated his Voudon-Gnostic artwork and theurgy into their higher curriculum[cite: 2].
