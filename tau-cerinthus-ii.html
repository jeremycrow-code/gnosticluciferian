In Memoriam: Tau Cerinthus II

THE MOST REV. +CARL EMMANUEL (1938–2011) // METROPOLITAN BISHOP & ESOTERIC MENTOR

The Most Reverend +Carl Emmanuel, consecrated under the ecclesiastical nomen Tau Cerinthus II, was a vital 20th-century custodian of Independent Gnostic, Martinist, and chivalric lineages in North America. Hailing originally from the Caribbean island nation of Grenada, Bishop Emmanuel relocated to Canada and established himself as a foundational pillar of the independent esoteric community in Toronto, Ontario.

As a trailblazing Gnostic Bishop of colour in North America during his era, +Carl Emmanuel stood as a distinguished, barrier-breaking figure whose scholarship, warm mentorship, and quiet spiritual authority commanded universal respect across traditionally insular occult and ecclesiastical fraternities.

"O Thou Divine Source of all Light, Life, and Love, we remember before Thee Thy faithful servant and bishop, +Carl (Tau Cerinthus II), who laboured in Thy mystical vineyard and illuminated the path of initiation. Grant that his spiritual spark, liberated from the bonds of earthly limitation, may shine forever radiant within the Fullness of the Pleroma, united with the Holy Sophia and the Aeons of Light. Amen."

Ecclesiastical Authority & The 1991 Concordat

Bishop Emmanuel's primary episcopal descent was derived from Archbishop +Charles Nurse in Barbados within the Gnostic Church of the Glorious Christ, tracing tactile succession through Bishop Roger Pommery (Titular Bishop of Macheronte for the Americas and Caribbean) directly to French World Patriarch Robert Ambelain (Tau Jean III).

Serving as the Metropolitan Bishop for the Canadian jurisdiction, Tau Cerinthus II formally integrated this Caribbean-West Indies stream into the broader North American Independent Gnostic movement:

  • The 1991 College of Métropolitains Concordat: Established under the guidance of French esoteric luminary René Chambellant (Tau Renatus) and organized by The Most Rev. +Robert M. Cokinis (Tau Charles Harmonius II), this historic concordat recognized the Gnostic Church of the Glorious Christ under Bishop Carl Emmanuel as an equal, valid jurisdiction alongside The Most Rev. +Stephan A. Hoeller (Ecclesia Gnostica) and Archbishop +Ronald V. Cappello (Old Templar Church). Through reciprocal recognitions and sub conditione exchanges, the historic Syrian-Antiochene, French Neo-Gnostic, and Templar lines converged.
  • The Synod of 2000: On November 11, 2000, during the Holy Sacrifice of the Mass at the Chapelle de Saint Montsalvat in Villa Park, Illinois, Bishop Emmanuel joined fellow prelates—including +Stephan Hoeller (Tau Stephanus I)—in formally constituting the Saint Collège Électoral of the Église Gnostique Catholique Apostolique (Synode des États-Unis d'Amérique du Nord).
  • The Martinist & Theosophical Foundations: A dedicated initiate of the Ordre Martiniste et Synarchique, +Carl Emmanuel operated as a Free Initiator in Toronto. Concurrently, he served for 25 years as a lecturer, counselor, and President within the Theosophical Society in Toronto, bridging classical 19th-century occult research with practical sacerdotal work.

Mentorship & Sacerdotal Work

Rejecting the archetype of the detached mystic, Bishop Emmanuel dedicated considerable energy to direct community service, including urban youth outreach in Toronto. In his esoteric work, he maintained small, intensive mentorship groups, transmitting the holy orders and initiatory mysteries to dedicated students. Among those he trained was Jeremy Crow, whom Bishop Emmanuel mentored in one-on-one tutelage from 1999 onward, ordaining him to the Gnostic Priesthood on February 8, 2003, and appointing him to serve as primary officiant for the Toronto congregation through 2004.
